Research

Perfusable Tissue Constructs

Scaffold and flow-channel strategies for sustaining engineered tissue viability.

Project Goal

The major limitation of current cellular models is that they are 2D, limited by oxygen diffusion. This project aimed to develop a 3D scaffold that supports diffusion to enable more physiologically relevant cell culture. We created a porous, perfusable scaffold to support cell growth and overcome diffusion constraints.

Approach

A gelatin matrix was engineered with porogens and embedded printing of sacrificial material to create channels. The scaffold was then placed in water to leech out the porogens, leaving behind pores which facilitate diffusion. The sacrificial channels were first perfused with water to clean them, then perfused with a red dye to monitor diffusion and demonstrate functionality.

Analysis

Cross-sectional slices were taken using a vibratome and analyzed with ImageJ to quantify the extent of dye diffusion throughout the scaffold, validating our 3D perfusion strategy.
